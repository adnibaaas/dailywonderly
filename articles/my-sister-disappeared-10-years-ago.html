My Sister Disappeared 10 Years Ago—Then Someone Sent Me a Photo Taken Three Days Ago

For ten years, Anna believed her older sister had vanished without a trace. Then an unknown number sent six words that made everything her parents had told her begin to fall apart.

By DailyWonderly Editorial September 2, 2026 7 min read
Editor's note This is an original fictional short story created for DailyWonderly. Characters and events are fictional.

At 11:47 on a Tuesday night, Anna's phone vibrated against her bedside table.

She almost ignored it.

The number wasn't saved.

There was no name.

Just one message.

Your sister never disappeared.

Anna stared at those words until the screen began to dim.

Her sister, Claire, had been missing for ten years.

At least, that was what Anna had always been told.

Claire vanished when Anna was seventeen

Claire was seven years older than Anna.

Growing up, she had been less like a sister and more like a second mother.

She helped Anna with homework.

She taught her how to drive.

Whenever their parents argued, Claire was the person who slipped into Anna's room and turned up the television so she wouldn't have to listen.

Then, one October morning, Claire was gone.

Her bedroom was empty.

Her car was gone.

Her phone had been disconnected.

The explanation changed slightly over the following weeks.

Claire had left.

Claire needed space.

Claire didn't want to be found.

Eventually, Anna stopped asking.

Claire's photographs disappeared from the walls.

Her bedroom became an office.

Her name slowly became something nobody in the house said anymore.

Then another message appeared

Anna sat upright in bed.

Her fingers shook as she typed:

Who is this?

No answer.

She waited.

Then the phone vibrated again.

This time there were no words.

Only a photograph.

A woman stood beside a gray car wearing jeans, a dark coat and sunglasses.

Her hair was shorter.

Her face was older.

But Anna recognized her immediately.

Claire.

Alive.

Another message appeared.

Saturday. 4:16 PM.

The photograph was only three days old.

Then Anna noticed the house behind her

At first Anna thought someone was playing a cruel joke.

She enlarged the image and studied every detail.

Behind Claire stood a narrow brick house with a dark green front door.

A maple tree leaned over the driveway.

The mailbox had the number 18 painted in white.

Anna knew that house.

It stood directly across the street from her parents' home.

She drove to her parents' house before midnight

Her father opened the door wearing pajamas and reading glasses.

“Anna? What's wrong?”

She walked past him.

“I got a message.”

She held up her phone.

Her father looked at the photograph.

His entire expression changed.

He didn't ask who the woman was.

He didn't ask where Anna got the photo.

He didn't pretend not to recognize Claire.

Instead, he said two words.

“Delete it.”

Anna felt something inside her go cold.

“You knew.”

Her father sat down

For several seconds, he said nothing.

Then he removed his glasses.

“It's complicated.”

Anna laughed once.

There was nothing amused about it.

“I've thought my sister was missing for ten years.”

“Anna—”

“You watched me cry over her.”

Her father looked toward the staircase.

“Keep your voice down.”

“Why? Because she's across the street?”

His eyes snapped toward her.

That was when Anna realized something else.

Her father knew Claire was alive.

But he hadn't known she was across the street.

Then someone knocked on the door

Three slow knocks.

Neither of them moved.

Anna's father whispered:

“Don't open it.”

Anna stared at him.

Another knock came.

Then a voice from the other side.

“Anna?”

She froze.

She hadn't heard that voice in ten years.

But she knew it instantly.

Claire.

Anna opened the door

Claire stood beneath the porch light.

For a moment, neither sister spoke.

Anna had imagined this reunion hundreds of times.

She had imagined screaming.

Crying.

Running into Claire's arms.

Instead, the only words she managed were:

“Where have you been?”

Claire looked past her toward their father.

“You really never told her?”

Claire had not disappeared

Ten years earlier, Claire had discovered something that changed the way she saw their family.

Their father had been quietly taking money from an account their grandmother had created for both sisters.

Claire confronted him.

The argument became enormous.

Their mother begged Claire not to involve Anna.

Anna was seventeen and already struggling with the constant tension inside the house.

Claire agreed to leave temporarily.

She stayed with a friend in another city.

But temporary became permanent.

Whenever Claire tried to contact Anna, their parents convinced her Anna didn't want to hear from her.

Whenever Anna asked about Claire, they told her Claire wanted nothing to do with the family.

Two sisters had spent ten years believing the other had chosen silence.

But that wasn't why Claire came back

Anna pointed toward the house across the street.

“Why were you there?”

Claire hesitated.

“Because Mrs. Turner called me.”

Mrs. Turner had lived across from their parents for more than twenty years.

She had also been close friends with their grandmother.

Claire reached into her bag.

She removed a thick yellow envelope.

Anna's name was written across the front.

“Grandma left this with Mrs. Turner before she died,” Claire said.

“She told her to give it to me if I ever came back.”

Their father suddenly stood.

“Claire.”

Claire looked at him.

“No.”

Then she handed the envelope to Anna.

Inside was a bank statement and a letter

Anna unfolded the first page.

It showed an account opened when she was a child.

Her grandmother had deposited money into it for years.

Money intended for Anna's education.

Most of it was gone.

Beneath the statements was a handwritten letter.

Anna recognized her grandmother's handwriting.

The final sentence had been underlined twice.

If Anna ever believes Claire abandoned her, tell her the truth.

Anna read it again.

Then again.

Her father said nothing.

The strangest part came last

Anna finally looked at Claire.

“Were you the one texting me?”

Claire frowned.

“What?”

Anna showed her the messages.

Claire read them.

Her face changed.

“That wasn't me.”

Anna felt the same cold sensation again.

“Then who took the picture?”

The sisters looked toward the window.

Across the street, Mrs. Turner's porch light switched on.

Then Anna's phone vibrated one final time.

Now you know why she left.

A second message appeared immediately afterward.

Ask your father what happened to the rest of the money.

Anna didn't delete anything

That night couldn't repair ten years of silence.

There were too many lost birthdays.

Too many questions.

Too many conversations that should have happened years earlier.

But for the first time since she was seventeen, Anna stopped wondering whether Claire had simply forgotten her.

She hadn't.

The sisters sat together until sunrise.

They began reconstructing ten years of a story they had each been told differently.

Anna never discovered with complete certainty who sent that first photograph.

Mrs. Turner always denied it.

Claire believed her.

Anna wasn't so sure.

But Anna kept the photograph.

Not because it showed the sister she had lost.

Because it captured the final moment before Anna learned that sometimes the person who disappears from your life isn't always the person who chose to leave.
